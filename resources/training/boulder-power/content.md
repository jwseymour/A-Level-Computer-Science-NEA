# Bouldering Power Development

## Program Overview
This 4-week program is designed to systematically develop explosive power and dynamic movement capabilities for indoor bouldering. The program combines campus board training, dynamic movement drills, and proper recovery protocols to ensure safe and effective power development.

## Program Goals
- Develop explosive power for dynamic movements
- Improve contact strength and body tension
- Build confidence in dynamic movements
- Enhance power endurance
- Master proper landing and falling techniques

## Weekly Structure
- 3 training sessions per week
- 48-hour minimum rest between power sessions
- Dedicated recovery protocols
- Progressive intensity increase

## Training Components

### 1. Campus Board Training
- Proper warm-up required
- Focus on form before power
- Progressive difficulty
- Safety considerations:
  - Minimum 1 year of climbing experience
  - Injury-free shoulders and fingers
  - Proper shoulder engagement
  - Quality over quantity

### 2. Dynamic Movement Drills
#### Progression Levels:
1. Static Deadpoints
   - Controlled movements to precise holds
   - Focus on body tension
   - Minimal swing on contact

2. Small Dynos
   - Both hands leaving wall
   - Controlled trajectory
   - Soft, precise landings

3. Full Dynos
   - Maximum distance movements
   - Coordinated body positioning
   - Landing control

### 3. Recovery Protocol
- Essential for power development
- Focus on:
  - Proper cool-down
  - Antagonist training
  - Mobility work
  - Recovery nutrition

## Safety Considerations
- Complete proper warm-up
- Progress gradually
- Listen to your body
- Rest adequately between attempts
- Perfect technique before increasing power

## Progress Markers
- Clean execution of movements
- Confidence in dynamic moves
- Campus board progression
- Recovery quality
- Overall power output

## Nutrition Focus
- Pre-session fuel
- Inter-attempt energy
- Post-session recovery
- Hydration maintenance

## Common Mistakes to Avoid
1. Insufficient warm-up
2. Overtraining
3. Poor technique under fatigue
4. Inadequate rest between sessions
5. Neglecting recovery protocols

## Adaptation Guidelines
- Scale intensity based on experience
- Modify volume based on recovery
- Adjust progression rate to ability
- Focus on quality over quantity

## Post-Program
After completing this program, you should:
- Have improved power output
- Be more confident in dynamic movements
- Understand proper power training principles
- Have developed better body awareness
- Know your power training limits

Remember: Power training is intense and requires proper rest and recovery. Always prioritize form and safety over rapid progression.
